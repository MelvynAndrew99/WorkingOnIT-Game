# Codex: road puzzles and solvability proposal

Design hypothesis, not implementation or balanced targets.

Core: accept growth -> see additional purposeful demand -> patch with space -> observe the bottleneck -> reorganize -> serve more demand with the same district -> earn a visible next opportunity. Player controls when to grow. A working inefficient city is a legitimate resting point. Early friction should predominantly delay additional earnings, not drain savings on a universal timer. Emergency deadlines are a separate, clearly signposted mechanic; no new general crisis charges in the first slice.

Do not reward raw cars present, road length, or distance driven. Show completed visits/minute and income/minute alongside unserved demand, return access, and waiting per affected neighborhood. Coverage and safety constrain throughput; a neighborhood cannot be sacrificed invisibly to make averages look good. Journey visit payment remains once at service, while successful return is tracked for network reliability; changing payout timing would be a separate economy migration. Count real demand including unspawned/blocked households and capacity-denied outside requests, not just admitted cars.

Puzzles (scenario archetypes, not forced map stamps):
1. Local access: homes and shop on existing road, later freely placed additional homes/park. Any solution with actual safe trips works.
2. Shared junction: adding a second neighborhood sends conflicting journeys through one crossing. Solutions: short controlled junction; longer perimeter connector avoiding the conflict; second destination where unmet demand justifies it. Stops/lights must remain available as basic safety tools. Signals are not universally higher throughput than stops; compare under actual load.
3. Destination bottleneck: available road capacity but full store. Distinguish reserved/occupied slots from road queues. Add shop near underserved homes, move access away from a junction, or eventually upgrade destination capacity. Roads alone should not fix insufficient service capacity.
4. Outside arrivals: voluntary connection brings outside customers along local approach; separate a through route from local entrances or place another shop near gateway. First bounded demand preset, not endless autonomous scaling.
5. First density: opt-in home occupancy upgrade introduces extra households within existing footprint. Makes space valuable without mandatory demolition or fabricated crashes. Must release tool/land alternatives before a dependency blocks progress. Advanced one-way/wider roads later only after existing controls/layouts have demonstrated enough depth.

Every fixture must include two materially different solutions with currently available tools, full outward+return access for every source, affordable construction/recovery, and a no-growth stable case. Compare identical demand and incident seed, warm up traffic, evaluate multiple demand cycles. Growing backlog or indefinitely starved approach fails even if total throughput rises. Tests cannot prove fun; ask playtesters to identify bottleneck, explain why their edit helped, and choose to improve again.

Keep a reference inefficient layout as a viable baseline. Optional compactness/road-tile comparisons can motivate optimization without upkeep debt. Do not impose compactness as universal score: a long bypass can be more effective. Good foresight can meet next objective immediately. No forced accident quota.

Proposed first bounded delivery: diagnostic unmet-demand/access/capacity distinctions plus one non-timed shared-junction growth objective with small claimable reward; measure before/after. Do not simultaneously ship new currencies, upgrade trees, driving styles and disaster economy. If goal-preservation outside the tutorial remains incomplete, fix it first because metrics cannot rescue wrong destinations.

Research: Dinosaur Polo Club's Mini Motorways page describes road redesign and managing upgrades against changing demand (https://dinopoloclub.com/games/mini-motorways/). Wube's FFF416 identifies unpredictable throughput and build-order dependence as frustrating (https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-416); our inference is to make road bottlenecks legible, not copy its removal of fluid routing. Supercell's Clan Capital/Forge description ties hall progression to available building upgrades (https://supercell.com/en/games/clashofclans/blog/game-updates/capital-forge/); borrow visible milestones, not waiting timers or its multiplayer structure.

Cross-review questions: Does successful-visit income already reward proximity strongly enough, making explicit efficiency multiplier redundant? Does unlimited park/recovery income let idle play dominate? How prevent unlocked-land requirements from softlocking a completely filled map? How does destination demand avoid every home/shop becoming an isolated optimal pod while still allowing that design as a valid early solution? Should the first growth unlock be occupancy or a new trip purpose? Propose the smallest test, not a full future simulation.
