I’ll research how successful-journey economies fund growth without grind, then write the economy/incentives roundtable piece against the no-rush and pathfinding constraints.Pulling a few primary design sources next: throughput-as-reward, visible progression without timers, and traffic-capacity as the real cost of space.**Economy: receipts of useful round-trips, not a clock**

Working ON IT! should pay for completed, *intended* work: a household leaves home, parks at the store it meant to visit, and returns. That event is Factorio-style productivity—more output from the same inputs—not time survived ([Factorio Wiki, Productivity](https://wiki.factorio.com/Productivity)). Wube capped recipe productivity at 300% and recycler return at 25% so loops cannot print net resources ([FFF-375](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-375)). Our analog: never pay twice for one parked visit, never let a park stand in for a blocked shop, never let refunds plus visit cash form a recycle loop.

Clash of Clans supplies the *skyline*, not the stopwatch. Steal Clan Capital: “There are no build times… when the requisite amount of Capital Gold is invested… it will be completed immediately,” wallet capped at 35,000 so a full balance blocks further earning, and raid losses do not steal Capital Gold ([Clash of Clans Wiki, Clan Capital](https://clashofclans.fandom.com/wiki/Clan_Capital) / [Raid Weekends](https://clashofclans.fandom.com/wiki/Raid_Weekends)). Home-village multi-day builder queues are the anti-pattern. Visible long-term progression here is the next land edge, clinic, and new town—goals you can see and choose to fund. Construction stays instant once paid, matching existing full paid-construction refunds.

Satisfactory’s pacing is the loop. Aphyr: no time limits and no quotas except those the player accepts; the constraint is attention, then rate, observability, and expandability ([A Satisfactory Way of Building](https://aphyr.com/posts/351-a-satisfactory-way-of-building)). Observe a queue, change a junction, watch receipts rise. Do not hang timers on ordinary trips, construction, or expansions just because an EMS scene has duration. Incident time is physics. Recovery allowance is insurance. A city-wide deadline is a different game.

**Do not copy OpenTTD’s payment shape.** Cargo income is rate × amount × Manhattan distance × time-in-transit; the wiki even notes you can earn more by seating stations on far sides of industries ([OpenTTD Wiki, Cargo income](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Game%20Mechanics/Cargo%20income)). Distance bonuses reward spaghetti; transit penalties are stealth deadlines. Pay a flat (provisional) receipt per completed intended round-trip. A later uncongested-completion bonus is optional. Not early.

**Space is the efficiency tax.** Mini Motorways’ designers: frequency is not the constraint; cars occupy space; a long road has high capacity but more shared routes and more intersections, so throughput falls ([Dinosaur Polo Club interview, Game Developer, 2021](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/-i-mini-motorways-i-and-the-delicate-art-of-marrying-complexity-and-minimalism)). Early ample tiles let a sloppy loop work. Each new home is the player opting into more simultaneous reservations. Roads cost cash *and* tiles later density will need. Full refunds make foresight and redesign cheap; a bad layout costs occupied capacity and delayed receipts, not a demolition fine. FFF-375’s horizontal vs vertical growth maps cleanly: more homes is horizontal; a cleaner junction is vertical. With no density upgrades, buses, or wider/one-way roads yet, vertical play *is* the road puzzle.

**Buildings vs incentives (all numbers provisional).** Home 200 / store 400 is directionally right: the store is parking sink and register. Park 300 must not be a cheaper destination that “clears” demand. Parks generate no shopping cash until leisure-patience is a real mechanic; they are amenity and later dwell, not a substitute register. Clinic 800 and new town 900 are skyline spends, funded by visit receipts, completed instantly. Road 20 vs home 200 implies a rough ~10-tile network budget per house—early maps can exceed that because space is ample. Stop 25 vs light 75: a light should beat a detour only at a contested pinch. If lights are always cheaper than eight extra tiles, the manager’s “more/bigger roads” line is always optimal and the puzzle dies. Starting money after Skip must still afford first home + first store + a short road, or the economy gates the tutorial’s own objective.

**Anti-spam / anti-idle.** Concurrent work is already bounded by cars, parking reservations, and road occupancy. Demand must not be a spawn mill: after a paid round-trip, that household dwells before the next shopping intent. That is backpressure (Aphyr: when downstream is full, upstream should slow), not a deadline. AFK with the sim running finishes in-flight trips, then idles. A paused city (manager briefings already pause simulation) mints nothing. No pay on placement. Outside arrivals are load unless they complete a city-intended stop. A hard wallet cap can wait: unused cash should see a land/clinic sink, not punish saving.

**How the three fit.** Buildings create intended demand and parking. Roads and controls are the belts: they decide which reserved paths actually enter tiles. The economy scores only completed intended loops. Player-chosen growth tightens the same puzzle. A working bypass is preserved because refunds are full and crashes are not spawned to punish success. The manager can still love bigger roads; the ledger should sometimes prove a stop or a leftover corridor was the better spend.

**Early puzzle (post-H, ample space).** Two player-built homes south of one store; a north–south arterial already carrying a capacity-aware outside arrival; one pinch where a side street meets that arterial at the store driveway. Both families must shop and return. All of these should work: (1) long shared two-way loop around the pinch (~8–12 extra road tiles, provisional); (2) a stop-controlled T (25 vs those tiles); (3) a dedicated return spur so outbound through-traffic never shares the driveway; (4) leave a one-tile future-bypass corridor and accept a longer first path. A park in the blocked driveway must not count. Manager stays quiet unless the queue is actually stuck past delayed-advice.

**Exploits / softlocks.** Park-for-shop substitution. Refund + rebuild resetting incident/waiver or double-paying a visit. AFK trip mill if dwell is missing. Reachable-destination filtering hiding demand (keep the goal path for reservation/save). Outside cars occupying the only EMS work tile. Expansion entitlement without visit evidence. Deadlock: reservations held, cars never enter, receipts never arrive, cannot afford a bypass—refunds plus recovery allowance must still let the player bulldoze a path. Manager hints must not grant cash. Skip must not leave a town that cannot afford the first store. Recovery allowance must be per-incident, not per-hour, or looping wrecks print money.

**Smallest next slice.** Ledger and HUD only: every cash tick tagged `visit-roundtrip | recovery-allowance | construction-refund | other`, with household id and intended destination. Assert blocked shopping ≠ park income; paused city = 0 receipts; refund does not pay a visit. No number retune, no new timers. Then a provisional post-trip dwell so overnight AFK cannot mint an expansion.

**Disagreements / questions (no claimed agreement).**
1. Buildings: any park cash at all before leisure-patience? I say no.
2. Road puzzles: do not gate the first expansion on a crash or timed clearance; ordinary shopping should fund growth.
3. Is Skip starting money enough for home+store+short road, or a tagged tutorial grant?
4. Uncongested-completion bonus: I lean no early—it is a stealth speed mandate.
5. Outside arrivals: pay only if they complete a city-intended stop.
6. Manager “credit”: never a multiplier for obeying advice, or the voice becomes a pay-to-obey exploit.
7. Police/EMS/fire severity still needs review: if fines or downtime scale with delay, that reintroduces city-wide timers through the back door.
