## Recommended immediate rules

Use lightweight, saved **rider tokens** in stop queues and bus loads. A token needs origin, destination, stage and a return destination; it does not need a pedestrian sprite, world path or per-frame pedestrian movement. Actual buses remain physical. An abstract rider is a representative transport request, not a census person or a proven shopping/customer receipt.

1. **Coverage:** assign each home to one nearest usable stop within six connected sidewalk road tiles; ties use stable stop ID. Walking adjacency is bidirectional beside one-way roads. Bus route legs obey driving direction. Empty gaps/buildings do not provide access. The depot can remain an interchange under the implemented contract.
2. **Useful service:** require a running physical bus and a legal complete route linking a home catchment with a different stop serving a store/park (or the explicitly connected gateway). Stops in empty fields do not generate customers. Validate return reachability as well as the outbound segment.
3. **Opening demand:** make one rider per served home ready immediately when it first gains useful service. Record that household's opening seed so duplicate routes, edits, stopping/starting or reloading cannot repeatedly seed it. The first bus collects these on its actual stop visit.
4. **Ongoing demand:** provisionally one new outbound request per home per 20 simulated seconds, capped at **two outstanding abstract round trips per home**, including queued, onboard, visiting and returning stages. This is a lead refinement of Grok's queued-only cap, preventing unlimited in-flight population. Unmet demand remains visible as unserved homes/blocked requests; caps are explicit saturation, not hidden success. Four served homes provide four opening riders and up to eight outstanding requests; more homes increase the visible backlog.
5. **Destinations:** select only buildings served by a different reachable stop. Use stable weighted rotation (two shopping requests for one park request where both exist), then lowest outstanding demand with stable IDs within a purpose. Do not always prioritize stores: that would starve parks, contrary to Grok's growth example. Keep destination intent fixed after admission.
6. **At a stop:** alight riders whose destination is here; then board eligible queued riders oldest first, up to **eight total seats**. A rider boards only a bus that will reach its destination. Actual bus movement and stop arrival change the load. Keep current dwell formula: `min(4, 1 + .25 × (boarded + alighted))` seconds. Four seconds caps duration, not the number of passenger events.
7. **Return flow:** alighting to a store/park triggers a representative 5/10-second stay, followed by a return request to the origin stop. Riders remain seated through the depot if their next stop is still ahead in the repeating loop. Complete their transport request only on arrival at the origin stop. Counters mean transport activity, not completed current-game shopping or home-arrival credit.
8. **Destination congestion:** waiting at the stop is distinct from using a shop. Keep the current 4/8 activity limits for linked car/walking journeys. The abstract prototype has a separate bounded representative stay pool (provisionally store4/park8); excess alighters wait visibly for that pool, with no shop reward. Do not combine the two pools into a claim of extra real service. Their eventual integration must share actual destination capacity.
9. **Sharing:** all routes and overlapping stop catchments share each building's demand budget. A second bus shares queues and can clear them more often; it does not create extra demand merely by being purchased. Cap total token records (provisionally256), preserve admitted return requests, and count denied new generation as unserved rather than successful travel.
10. **Disruption/removal:** a broken route stops new outbound admission and reports why. Keep queued/onboard tokens and waits; resume after repair. Guard deletion of dependent stops while occupied buses return. A removed origin/destination cancels its unfinished requests without credits; onboard cancellations clear at a real depot/served stop and increment an explicit canceled count. Do not silently delete excess returners or riders at arbitrary road positions.
11. **Gateway:** use the existing explicit gateway coordinate, not a nonexistent gateway building. Share its current opportunity clock/live limit; route eligible opportunities through a served stop, with a real bus return leg toward that gateway stop. Do not add a second invisible visitor generator alongside existing car arrivals. This may follow the home/store prototype if the shared admission boundary is not ready.
12. **Persistence/UI:** save token IDs, stage/destination, shared demand clocks/seed receipts, queues, onboard ownership and stay timers. Existing `4 waiting` / `6/8` indicators show these actual abstract counts. Show route-blocked/unserved feedback, no new timetable or mode menu. No individual pedestrian rendering required.

## Coexistence with implemented journeys

Do not delete or reset existing real passengers to introduce this prototype. Already admitted linked journeys retain their rewards and physical returns. A service can drain its existing linked riders before switching its admission source internally; no user-facing simulation-mode toggle is needed. If mixed operation is retained, abstract riders must respect all already reserved linked seat intervals, and the combined load may never exceed eight. Abstract counters never count as real household trips, service receipts, crash victims or car reductions. Keep abstract vs linked counts distinct internally even if both use the same bus load display.

The future step is replacing abstract request generation with household demand assignments, then evidencing fewer actual cars for equivalent real service. Forcing a real household to choose a bus is not inherently fake, contrary to Grok's blanket wording; it simply is a separate, larger coupling/balance change. It is not required for this user's immediate visible-ridership prototype.

## Economics recommendation

Retain provisional **depot $1,000, stop $50, bus $400** for initial testing: two stops and one bus cost **$1,500**, with at most two buses per depot. Keep **no fare and no upkeep** during this detached prototype. It supplies visible route activity but has no proven positive economic return or congestion benefit until linked; do not market these prices as balanced. Existing shopping income, support and fixed-budget challenges remain unchanged. Abstract drop-offs do not earn the existing $100 shopping payment, park benefit, growth permits or mission stars.

An eventual transport-payment rule can be separate and bounded, paid only once on a completed origin-return request, with a shared town-level cap. Grok floated $1 per return capped at $10 per ten seconds, but recommends deferring it. No fare/grant/upkeep numbers are adopted here. A balance decision should consider the reward for real service/car reduction rather than paying arbitrary occupancy.

**Illustrative arithmetic only:** with a hypothetical 60-second loop, eight seats permit up to eight outbound riders on the home→destination segment and eight return riders on the destination→home segment after the pipeline fills. This is at most eight completed round trips per minute under ideal stop spacing and no disruptions, not sixteen round trips. Four homes at the nominal 1/20s clock request twelve departures/minute before the two-outstanding cap; one bus can therefore develop a backlog and a second bus can matter. Actual trip timing, dwell, depot waits, stay times and shared seats lower/shape this ideal bound. No benchmark or observed-load claim is made.

## Corrections to Grok's raw review

- Sidewalk reach is not constrained by one-way car direction.
- Depot boarding/interchange is already part of the bus contract, not forbidden.
- Keep explicit gateway consent/opportunities and returning visitors.
- A four-second dwell cap does not imply a twelve-event boarding cap.
- Strict store-first selection does not provide the claimed store/park split.
- Do not silently discard over-cap returners, broken-route queues or onboard people. Retain admitted requests or report cancellation explicitly.
- The sample timetable is illustrative and compresses several stop visits; it does not prove a particular load on the second lap.

## Implementation acceptance

Empty-land routes have zero queue/load. Four-home/store routes show opening demand and actual boarding, unloading, stay and return counts. Additional homes produce more demand/backlog; additional destinations distribute service. Duplicate stops/routes do not multiply supply. Seat count never exceeds eight; full buses leave waiting counts. Blocked routes preserve requests across reload and recover after repair. Full representative destinations queue arrivals. Start/stop/reload does not reseed. Already linked journeys survive migration. Abstract service produces no existing shop/mission/return rewards and does not remove cars. Verify desktop/narrow counters when implemented; performance measurements remain on user request only.
