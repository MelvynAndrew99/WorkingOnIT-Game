## Concrete art assignment

The assigned art/UI specialist should first deliver a local candidate sheet and editable source in `docs/artwork/transit/`, with a short inventory/provenance README. Suggested files: `generate.mjs`, `review.html`, `comparison.png`, `station-{N,E,S,W}.png`, `stop-{N,E,S,W}.png`, `bus-{N,E,S,W}.png`. This is the next production handoff, not a claim that those files exist or a specialist has delivered them. Preserve existing candidate/adopted art separately. Prefer composing the inspected pack and extending the established pixel-art system; only the missing transit identity/details and bus body need new drawing.

- **Station:** provisional 3×3 logical tiles, 48×48 native pixels for each orientation. Reserve visibly open forecourt space for bus parking, with a modest terminal occupying only part of the footprint. Its entire nine-tile lot blocks other construction. Parking belongs inside this lot, not on adjacent grass or public road. The entrance and interior bay access must follow the simulation's eventual geometry; draw only the actual public-road access. The facade remains upright when the entrance changes. This size is comparable to the existing 3×3 park and larger than 3×2 service buildings; change it in concert with the transit contract before final exports if larger bus clearance requires it.
- **Stop:** exactly 1×1 occupied off-road tile, provisionally 16×16 native pixels. Include a high-contrast bus pictogram/pole and small waiting pavement. Show the boarding edge toward its assigned adjacent road; keep graphics inside the tile for the first version, so a shelter does not conceal adjoining traffic or imply extra occupied squares. N/E/S/W refer to the road-facing boarding side; they are separate upright drawings/compositions, not a rotating facade. The adjacent road remains a road. An in-lane dwelling bus holds that road lane, not the stop tile.
- **Bus:** four fixed-viewpoint views; E/W show the side, N the rear, S the front. Use a recognizably longer cabin with several windows and a distinct front/door location, without emergency colors or flashing beacons. Proposed working canvases are 48×24 for E/W and 22×29 for N/S, with transparent margins and explicit wheel/contact anchors. These are export canvases, not collision dimensions. First compare beside the current civilian car and approved fire engine. The transit owner selects a compact one-tile physical envelope for the first prototype; prepare a matching compact visual variant. A 1.3–1.5-tile side-view body is a later candidate blocked on swept-tail, turning, passing and merge occupancy support and tests. Do not let the compact prototype visually advertise a long body that has no corresponding collision space, and do not stretch every current vehicle through the shared `VEHICLE_WIDTH` table. Keep direction changes visually stable with shared anchors.
- **Parking occupancy:** export the station empty. Render its actual parked fleet independently from saved fleet state, using bus art at defined bay anchors. No decorative permanent bus that remains while the real fleet is away. Show overflow or unavailable parking through actual status, not extra painted bays silently granting capacity. The first station is a depot plus interchange with pedestrian boarding frontage. Passenger-car parking/park-and-ride is deferred; its parking bays serve the bus fleet.
- **Identity:** use a modest transit color accent shared by station, stop and bus, plus a bus silhouette so identity does not depend on color or small text. Avoid copying the police shield, medical cross or fire insignia. Route color/number is a live overlay where useful; a bus should still read as a bus without that overlay.

## One-way flow and access must remain distinguishable

The current renderer places a large green or amber arrow on a dark pad in the *road* access tile, pointing toward a building. That is an entrance marker, not driving direction. The supplied screenshots expose exactly this potential ambiguity.

Prototype persistent white lane-centered one-way arrows using the existing frames, with a selected-road outline and explicit direction text in inspection. Keep entrance markers at the building edge/apron, or offset them to a clearly separate access location when sharing a road tile. Preserve their real connection state and ensure both markers remain readable on narrow screens. Color alone is insufficient: flow follows the lane; access points across the edge toward the building. Stop boarding indication must not resemble a second driving direction.

Arrows on corners/junctions must depict the legal exit/turn contract chosen by the routing owner; do not display a straight arrow into grass or imply every branch is usable. Verify an eight-road ring around an empty central tile in both clockwise and counterclockwise configurations. Route-preview highlighting can show the connected cycle without adding a special roundabout object. Center dashes can remain existing texture details unless actual visual review finds them misleading; the user has requested direction markings, not a new road-art overhaul.

## Renderer and atlas integration contract

Keep model footprints, entrance positions, bus occupancy and stop dwell state independent of sprite bounds. Existing `entrance()` assumes a non-home entrance at local x=1; a one-tile stop needs explicit geometry support rather than inheriting that assumption. `buildingPieces()` and `vehicleView()` currently have closed kind unions and do not yet handle transit.

After candidate selection, integrate exports through `tools/build-city-atlas.mjs` with dimension validation and semantic frame names such as `building_busStation_N`, `busStop_N`, `bus_N`. Regenerate `src/game/cityAtlas.ts`; do not hand-edit frame rectangles. Keep the existing transparent gutter, nearest-neighbor sampling, manifest warm-up and missing-asset fallback. Store provenance and editable sources outside `public/`.

Keep ground/building/road-arrow geometry in static cached layers, invalidated on edits. Place parked/moving fleet, boarding progress and demand counts in separate pooled dynamic layers so bus occupancy does not rebuild the station texture every frame. Reuse style guards and avoid per-frame allocations or rebuilding every stop label. Base shadows/contact placement on bus dimensions; do not let a long bus's artwork hide the preceding car while its model still occupies a car-sized space.

## Visual acceptance and delivery evidence

Check only desktop and narrow layouts by default, using actual gameplay at ordinary zoom and the user's performance-town fixture in isolation. Include the following states in the two layouts rather than creating an unnecessary screen-size matrix:

1. All four station entrances and stop boarding sides; valid/invalid placement previews show the entire occupied footprint and the actual road tile needed.
2. Empty/partially occupied/full station, a bus entering/leaving, and a bus dwelling while real followers queue. Verify that parking and boarding are spatially legible and stay outside neighboring lots.
3. Both bus directions on a straight, a tight corner and the proposed ring, beside cars/responders. No rotated windshield, anchor jump, road-edge clipping or false free space behind a long bus.
4. One-way arrow beside a building entrance, stop, Stop sign and traffic light. Legal direction stays distinguishable without relying on color. Saved direction and art agree after reload and live road edits.
5. Stop waiting/boarding counts and route inspection remain legible without covering the adjacent lane; selected stop clearly identifies its boarding side. A full bus does not look like everyone boarded.
6. Measure actual frame pacing for the supplied 200-road/50-building town on desktop/narrow before and after representative transit load; compare rendering and simulation separately. Software-browser improvements do not establish smoothness on the user's real desktop. Retain the existing static caches.

Attach real candidate previews before requesting artwork selection, and record what was actually inspected in-game. Agent review is not approval.” This affects final artwork adoption; it does not prevent preparing the candidates or implementing independently testable systems.
