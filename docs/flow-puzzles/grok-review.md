Your correction is right: the civic ask should be a useful outcome in *this* town, exaggeration is fine, and a simple flow grade can nod at LOS without claiming HCM. ## First arc (same town, after tutorial)

**The Saturday strip.** A neighborhood you already built grows a handful of homes. A popular grocer/park on the far side of *one* connecting street becomes a weekend magnet. Bounded extra shoppers (locals + a finite pulse of visitors) all want to go, linger a bit, and get home before the window ends. The manager announces it like a triumph they planned.

**Player-facing outcome:** “Serve Saturday on Maple: most of these trips get to the strip *and back home* during the busy stretch.” Not “fix Maple,” not “unlock four lanes,” not “hit LOS C.” If they already built a second access, extra shops, or a short path, they can pass with little drama. That is a win, not a skipped lesson.

Cash/land stay as now. Old saves keep working. Pause, slow-mo, and refunds stay.

## Readable measurements (not a textbook)

Drop facility-specific LOS letters as a claim. Use a **street mood** the eye already believes:

- **Smooth / Busy / Crawling / Jam** on the *named* approach (inspired by A–F, no HCM badge).
- **Round trips completed this window** vs intended demand (the real score).
- **Typical time vs a pleasant trip** (e.g. 2× / 4×), shown on a few highlighted cars.
- A short **queue sausage** on the limiting street.

Cumulative “cars ever arrived” is the wrong receipt. Packed parking from stalled departures is a *fail* of the homeward half, not success.

## Two materially different solutions

1. **Split the load:** a parallel street, second driveway, or a timed junction that actually lets the return pulse out. Same destinations, two paths.
2. **Move/duplicate the magnet:** a second grocer or park on the neighborhood side so the surge never has to punch the same corridor.

Widening, if/when it exists, is a *third* spectacular hammer for *that* street only. It must not clear a downstream lot choke or a one-lane exit.

## Exaggerated but causal demand/capacity

Do not assume today’s idle traffic saturates a straight road. **Script a visible pulse:** overlapping school-out + market open, everyone leaves within a short beat, visits are finite, then it stops. Make cars chunky, gaps stingy, the queue obviously *on the street*. Parking stalls exist and cycle; they are sized so the street fills first if everyone uses one door. Returns matter: the outbound wave and the inbound wave can each clog Maple.

Growth pressure = a few new houses + one advertised Saturday, not a spawn loop that rewards success with more cars forever.

## Roads limiting, not parking

Give the lot enough stalls and a working exit. Score **departures home**, not occupancy. If the lot is full because nobody can leave, the mood on the exit street goes Crawling/Jam and round-trips tank. The manager’s funny bad advice: “Just add parking!” when the camera is staring at a street queue.

## Bounded growth, anti-cheese (keep it light)

- Finite visitor tickets for the window; success does not raise the spawn rate.
- No random crashes after a solved conflict.
- **Deleting demand fails the civic ask** (those households/visitors are the point).
- **Permanent red lights / infinite wait** fail round-trips.
- **Walling off the strip** fails service.
- One-way into a parking trap fails the way home.
- You may refund and rebuild; you may not win by erasing the neighborhood.

No new upkeep, no new currency, no practice district.

## Manager

Sincere, grand, credit-hungry. “Maple never stood a chance against *my* market.” Useful-wrong: more parking, one more lane of wishful thinking, “they can walk” while the camera shows strollers in gridlock. Funny, not a tutorial overlay that names the tool.

## Frank critique

The first sketch taught *widening* and borrowed authority from LOS. In a persistent city that already has junctions, alternate roads, and destination placement, that puzzle is both too prescribed and too easy to miss if the player already branched. Four-lane without lane choice/merges is also a visual upgrade that may not *play* different yet. Factorio-in-your-factory is the right instinct; **imposed failure after good planning is the wrong one**. Cumulative receipts will lie. Parking-as-score will lie. Endless escalation will turn a toy into a treadmill.

## Uncertain assumptions

- Can an exaggerated pulse actually queue a one-lane-per-direction street in *current* sim, or is the bottleneck still lots/junctions/pathing?
- Free-form towns may have no unique “Maple,” so the event must attach to *whatever* single access the strip has.
- Pause + refund may drain tension unless the payoff is the *visible* queue dying and the homeward cars rolling.
- Players may read “Busy” as punishment rather than a solvable Saturday.

**Smallest playtest:** one neighborhood, one strip, one connecting street, a 2–3 minute (or paused) Saturday pulse, goal ≈ 80% home–shop–home. No new road type. Watch faces, not grades: do they grin when the sausage shrinks after a bypass or a second shop? If the queue never forms, the puzzle is not ready, four lanes or not.

## Two routes (your call)

**Existing-tool prototype first.** Ship the civic *window* + round-trip score + street mood + Saturday pulse on current one-lane-per-direction. Prove the fun with bypass vs second magnet. Four-lane later as a loud toy that helps only the limiting link.

**Four-lane in the first arc.** Same civic ask; widening is a valid, juicy third solution. Budget lane-select/merges enough that a fat Maple is not a paint job. Still force a case where the lot/exit/downstream street stays the choke so “always widen” does not win.

I would not pick for you: the prototype is cheaper proof of *objective and feel*; four-lane is the postcard payoff if the sim can already show a street-limited queue.
